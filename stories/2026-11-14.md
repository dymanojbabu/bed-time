# The Paper Star

**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Theme:** being honest even when it is hard  
**Ages:** 6-7

---

The November wind hummed outside, but inside, the room was cozy and warm. Clara sat by the glowing lamp light with her mother. It was time for bed, but first, Clara had something heavy resting in her heart.

Earlier that afternoon, Clara had found a delicate paper star on the table. It had a number written on each of its five points—a little puzzle her mother loved. Clara had carried it into the playroom to see how gently it could fly. But when it drifted down, one of the crisp paper points had bent.

Clara had tucked the star away, wishing she could hide the mistake. All evening, her tummy felt like it was tied in a tight knot. Was it best to say nothing and pretend? *No*, a quiet voice inside whispered. 

Clara took a slow, deep breath. She reached into her pocket and pulled the paper star out.

"Mom?" Clara whispered, looking at her hands. "I bent your star. I wanted to see it fly, but I was not careful. I was scared to tell you, but I wanted to try."

Her mother looked at the bent point, then smiled softly. She pulled Clara into a warm hug. 

"Thank you for telling me the truth, sweet pea," her mother said. "It is hard to be honest when you feel afraid. But the truth is like a lantern. It helps us trust each other, and that is what matters most."

"You are not mad?" Clara asked quietly.

"I am proud of you," her mother answered. "Paper can be folded again. You were brave, and that is what counts."

Together, they smoothed the star flat and set it on the nightstand, where the moon shared its sleepy glow. Then, Clara’s mother opened a book to read a peaceful bedtime tale. Clara rested her head on her soft pillow. The tight knot in her tummy was completely gone, replaced by a warm, gentle calm. Outside, the autumn night was still, and Clara closed her eyes, drifting off to sweet, cozy dreams.
