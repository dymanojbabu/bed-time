# The Oak Tree Slide

**Date:** November 11, 2026  
**Theme:** taking turns and being patient  
**Ages:** 6-7

---

The November day was brisk and bright. Hazel the little squirrel pulled her soft acorn cap down over her ears. Right by the big oak tree, she saw her brother Pip dancing with excitement. 

"Look at this leaf slide!" cried Pip. 

A long, smooth bark branch leaned against a mossy hill. It made the best slide in the whole forest. But the log was narrow. They could see that only one little squirrel could fit at a time. 

"I want to go right now!" said Pip with a hop. Waiting felt very hard.

Hazel smiled. "We can take turns," she said gently. "You can start. Slide all the way down, and then it will be my turn."

Pip did a quick nod. He sat on the log and whooshed down. "Wheee!" he giggled at the bottom. "How does it feel to fly like a bird? Just like that!"

Now it was Hazel’s turn. Pip stood by the moss to wait. Because he wanted to be patient, Pip began to sing a sweet counting song while Hazel climbed to the top:

"One, two, red and blue,
Leaves are falling, just for you!"

Hazel zipped down the log with a happy squeak. She landed right into some soft, golden leaves. Pip clapped his little paws. Taking turns did not feel hard anymore. It made the game twice as fun because they could cheer for each other. Back and forth they went, sharing the slide until the sun began to dip behind the hills.

Soon, the evening air grew cold. The sky turned deep purple, and stars began to blink through the quiet branches. 

"Time to rest," yawned Hazel. 

Together, they scampered up into their warm hollow nest, filled with dried moss and fluffy feathers. 

"You are a good brother," Hazel whispered, snuggling close. 

Pip smiled softly as his heavy eyes closed. The forest was peaceful and still, resting under the starry November night, ready for sweet dreams.
